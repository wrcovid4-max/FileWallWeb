    Privacy & security

    No, and not because we promise not to look. There is no FileWall server for your files to arrive at, no account tying them to you, and no code path that sends them anywhere. We have no infrastructure that touches your data.

    If you turn on Drive backup, the archive goes to your Google Drive through your credentials, encrypted with your passphrase before it leaves the phone.

    Your files stay encrypted. Whoever has the phone needs to get past the device lock, and then past your passcode for the hidden archive. Pulling the app's data folder off the device gets them nothing usable: the keys live in the Keystore and do not come with the files.

    Getting your own files back is a different question, and the answer is a backup. If you have an archive and its passphrase, you can restore onto a new phone. If you don't, the files are gone with the phone.

    The hidden archive locks you out for a while after five wrong attempts, and the wait gets longer each time it happens. It is there to make guessing a four-digit code expensive rather than instant.

    Backup & restore

    They do different jobs. The PIN gates the hidden archive on this device; it is checked against a PBKDF2 hash and never unlocks a file by itself, because the file keys are in the Keystore.

    A backup has to survive somewhere that Keystore doesn't exist — a Drive folder, an SD card, another phone. So the archive is keyed from a passphrase you supply, stretched with PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 at 210,000 iterations. Without that passphrase the archive is bytes.

    They go with it. Uninstalling removes the app's private storage — the encrypted files and the database holding their names, types and folders. Android does not keep a copy for you, and neither do we.

    Export what you want to keep first, or take a backup archive. Files you exported earlier stay wherever you put them.

    Take a backup archive on the old phone, choose a passphrase, and keep the file somewhere you control — a Drive folder, an SD card, a laptop. On the new phone, install FileWall and restore from that archive with the same passphrase.

    Restores are verified before anything is written, so a truncated or tampered archive fails loudly instead of half-importing.

    Files you export are ordinary files — they leave the vault decrypted and open in whatever app you like. That is the escape hatch, and it is deliberate.

    A backup archive is different: it is encrypted with your passphrase and is meant to be restored by FileWall, not opened by hand.

    Platforms

    Because the guarantees come from the platform itself: keys confined to the Keystore, the system file picker that removes the need for a storage permission, and the flag that keeps the vault out of screenshots and the app switcher. A cross-platform shell would have to give up some of that, so each platform gets a version built against its own secure hardware instead.
