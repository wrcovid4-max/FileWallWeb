    FileWall Arrives on iOS, iPadOS and Apple Watch in Early Beta

    The private, on-device vault comes to Apple devices — encrypted on the phone, and now in your pocket, on your desk and on your wrist.

    Aug 16, 2026

    Today we're bringing FileWall — the private, on-device vault — to Apple devices. FileWall is now in early beta on iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch, the first step toward a vault that follows you across the Apple ecosystem.

    Your files tend to scatter: photos here, documents there, downloads and archives somewhere else again. FileWall is built around a straightforward idea — your files should be easy to find and easy to organise, and they should stay private while you do it. Everything you add is encrypted on the device and lives in a vault only you can open. There is no account, and there is no server behind it.

    Built for your Apple devices

    FileWall is designed from the ground up for Apple's hardware, so each version unlocks with the security your device already has and makes the most of its screen.

    📱iPhone

    On iPhone, FileWall gives your files a dedicated, private home. Browse documents, organise folders and open what you saved earlier — all behind Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode, with a hidden vault tucked behind a second passcode for the things that need it most.

    🖥iPad

    File management gets more room on iPad. A permanent sidebar and a side-by-side view make working with larger collections comfortable, and Markup lets you annotate a photo or document without it ever leaving the vault. It's a file manager that feels at home on a bigger screen, not a stretched phone app.

    ⌚Apple Watch

    FileWall is making its way to Apple Watch too. The watch is built around quick access rather than the whole file manager: a glance at your unlocked files, right from your wrist. Hidden files are never sent to the watch, so a lost watch can't even ask for them.

    Designed around simplicity

    File management doesn't need to feel complicated. FileWall keeps the experience organised and understandable, and focuses on the things people actually need:

    • Browse your files, encrypted on the device
    • Organise folders and content
    • Find files more easily
    • Manage photos, videos, documents and more
    • Keep a second, hidden vault behind its own passcode
    • Access your files across supported Apple devices

    The aim is simple: open FileWall and immediately understand where everything is — while knowing it's all encrypted underneath.

    Early beta: the beginning, not the finish line

    FileWall is in early beta, and that matters. This first version is the foundation. Some features are still evolving, the interface will keep changing, and more capabilities are planned as development continues. Rather than presenting a finished product, we're opening the door early and building the experience step by step, guided by real-world use.

    One app, several Apple platforms

    FileWall isn't being designed around a single device. You might save a document on your iPhone, organise on your iPad, and glance at something on your Apple Watch — so FileWall is built with all three in mind from the start. The goal is one consistent, private experience, with each version making the most of what its device does best.

    Built with a bigger vision

    FileWall started with a simple question: what if keeping your files private on Apple devices could feel this simple? A vault shouldn't feel like an afterthought — it should feel like a proper part of your everyday workflow, from the iPhone in your pocket to the iPad on your desk and the Apple Watch on your wrist.

    The journey starts now

    FileWall is entering early beta on iOS, iPadOS and Apple Watch. This release is the foundation for everything that comes next. There's still a lot to build and refine — but this is where FileWall begins.

    FileWall — your private vault for the files you keep close. Available now in early beta for iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch.
